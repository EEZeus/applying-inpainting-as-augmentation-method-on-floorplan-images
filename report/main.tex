\begin{abstract}
Room-instance segmentation of architectural floor plans is limited by class
imbalance. In the 56-plan, 19-category dataset used here, six primary-rare room
categories contribute 70 of 1633 polygon annotations and only 17 test instances.
This report asks whether generating additional rare-room examples with diffusion
inpainting repairs that imbalance, and whether the answer depends on the
detection backbone. Fourteen augmentation arms were built from the training
split alone. They cover prompt-based Stable Diffusion 1.5 inpainting and a
rank-8 LoRA adapter, plus an exemplar-guided variant with copy-paste and
feather-blend controls. Each arm was evaluated with a torchvision Mask R-CNN over three
seeds and a rotated held-out cross-validation, and compared against a fine-tuned
SAM 3 and a two-stage pipeline that labels class-agnostic proposals with a
frozen DINOv2 classifier. An audit found that the cross-validation reused the
checkpoint-selection images and that augmented arms saw real plans less often
than the baseline. A pre-registered confirmatory study repaired these defects
and re-tested the surviving signals with 12 fresh seeds per arm. Exemplar
inpainting changed class-agnostic mask AP by $-0.004$ (95\,\% CI $-0.019$ to
$+0.009$), and plain copy-paste gained $+0.011$ (CI $-0.007$ to $+0.029$).
Rare-class AP stayed at or near zero for every augmentation, and the DINOv2
classifier recalled no rare room even on ground-truth crops.

The plans print the room name inside most rooms, and a final set of
experiments read it with OCR. In a pre-registered study a text branch in the Mask R-CNN
classifier, trained with the detector's schedule, barely left its zero
initialisation and gave no gain. Given its own optimiser in an exploratory
follow-up, the same branch raised multiclass mask AP from $0.100$ to $0.184$
(12 seeds, 95\,\% CI of the gain $+0.070$ to $+0.112$). An OCR text classifier
applied to class-agnostic SAM 3 masks reached $0.258$ multiclass AP on the test
plans, against $0.115$ for SAM 3 alone. Erasing printed names by inpainting and
rendering rare-class names in their place had no measurable effect, and
copy-paste of rare rooms towards class balance lowered the detector's AP by up
to $0.017$, although the same synthetic rooms raised the rare-class AP of the
text classifier from $0.099$ to $0.126$.
Room localisation is already solved to $0.59$--$0.71$ class-agnostic AP on this
data. Room classification depends on the printed label, which inpainting
augmentation of the training pixels does not supply.
\end{abstract}
