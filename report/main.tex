\begin{abstract}
Room-instance segmentation of architectural floor plans is limited by class
imbalance. In the 56-plan, 19-category dataset used here, six primary-rare room
categories contribute 70 of 1633 polygon annotations and only 17 test instances.
This report asks whether generating additional rare-room examples with diffusion
inpainting repairs that imbalance, and whether the answer depends on the
detection backbone. Fourteen augmentation arms were built from the training
split alone, covering prompt-based Stable Diffusion 1.5 inpainting, an
exemplar-guided variant with copy-paste and feather-blend controls, and a rank-8
LoRA adapter. Each arm was evaluated with a torchvision Mask R-CNN over three
seeds and a rotated held-out cross-validation, and compared against a fine-tuned
SAM 3 and a two-stage pipeline that labels class-agnostic proposals with a
frozen DINOv2 classifier. An audit of that evaluation found that the
cross-validation reused the checkpoint-selection images, that augmented arms saw
real plans less often than the baseline, and that the diffusion arm generated
almost no pixels. A pre-registered confirmatory study repaired these defects and
re-tested the surviving signals with 12 fresh seeds per arm. Exemplar inpainting
with a diffusion band of three latent cells changed class-agnostic mask AP by
$-0.004$ (95\,\% CI $-0.019$ to $+0.009$), which excludes a practically relevant
gain, and plain copy-paste, the strongest arm of the original grid at $+0.036$,
gained only $+0.011$ (CI $-0.007$ to $+0.029$). A multiclass follow-up found Strategy A, prompt-based inpainting of rare rooms, ahead of the baseline by $+0.006$ AP (one-sided seed-level $p = 0.034$), but its seed-and-image interval ($-0.004$ to $+0.019$) includes zero, so the gain is not established. Rare-class
AP stayed at or near zero for every method, and the frozen DINOv2 classifier
recalled zero rare instances even on perfect ground-truth crops. The dominant
effect was the backbone: SAM 3 exceeded the best Mask R-CNN arm by $0.089$
class-agnostic AP, about four times the typical augmentation effect.
Class-agnostic room localisation is already solved to $0.59$--$0.71$ AP. The
unsolved problem is room-type classification at this data scale, and inpainting
augmentation does not address it.
\end{abstract}
