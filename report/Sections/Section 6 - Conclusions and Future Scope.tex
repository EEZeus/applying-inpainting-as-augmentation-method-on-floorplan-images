\section{Conclusions and Future Scope}

The project set out to determine whether diffusion or LoRA inpainting of rare
room types improves room-instance segmentation of architectural floor plans, and
whether the answer depends on the backbone. The measured answer is that it does
not improve segmentation, and that the backbone matters far more than the
augmentation.

Goal 1 was achieved as specified. Fourteen controlled arms were built on
generators that read only frozen training images, with each accepted sample
passing automated geometry, pixel-quality and structural checks and carrying a
recorded audit. Each arm trained under the same model, schedule and step budget
in both views. The matrix included the non-generative controls that make the
diffusion treatment interpretable, and that design choice, rather than any
positive finding, produced the project's most useful result.

Goal 2 was achieved in the sense its statement anticipated. A defensible answer
was obtained, and the answer is a null result. Across 84 Mask R-CNN training
runs, no augmentation arm produced a difference from the unaugmented baseline
that the data supports. Every paired sign test on the three-seed grid returned
$p \ge 0.25$, the floor of the test at $n=3$. Nine of the thirteen augmented
arms scored below baseline on the class-agnostic track, with the LoRA conversion
worst at $-0.057$. The cross-validation over 21 held-out images reproduced the
same ordering. The single result with a consistent sign is exemplar copy-paste
at $+0.045$ class-agnostic AP, positive in all five folds at $p = 0.0625$, and
copy-paste is the control arm that contains no generated pixels. That outcome
runs against the hypothesis under test, and Ghiasi et al.\
\cite{ghiasi2021copypaste} would have predicted it.

Goal 3 changed the framing of the project. SAM 3 without any augmentation
reached $0.713$ class-agnostic and $0.115$ multiclass mask AP, exceeding the
best Mask R-CNN arm by $0.089$ and $0.011$ respectively. The class-agnostic gap
is about two and a half times the largest augmentation delta measured anywhere
in the grid. Under augmentation SAM 3 showed a $+0.032$ multiclass gain from
copy-paste, but on a single seed, inside the $0.002$ to $0.018$ seed-noise band
measured on the grid, and accompanied by a class-agnostic loss under all three
augmentations. The sign of the copy-paste effect is therefore opposite in the
two backbones. That inconsistency is itself the finding: at this data scale the
augmentation effect is not a stable property of the augmentation.

Goal 4 supplied the diagnosis. Handed perfect ground-truth crops, with no
detector error whatsoever, a frozen DINOv2-base encoder with a linear head
yielded $0.454$ top-1 accuracy over 19 classes and exactly $0.000$ macro recall
over the six primary-rare classes, in every arm and for both heads. Adding the
171 synthetic rare crops lowered top-1 accuracy in both heads and left rare
recall at zero. The end-to-end two-stage system reached $0.091$ mask AP, below
both fine-tuned detectors. Combined with the class-agnostic results, this
locates the failure precisely. Room localisation is largely solved on this
dataset at $0.59$ to $0.71$ AP, while room typing is not, for the tail and for
several head classes as well. No augmentation of the training pixels was going
to fix a classification problem of that shape.

The wider conclusion is a scope statement rather than a defeat. The generative
augmentation literature surveyed in Section~3 reports its gains on benchmarks
with 100k training images, where a rare class still has a few hundred instances
\cite{gupta2019lvis,xie2024mosaicfusion,shin2023fillup}. This project had 35
training plans, 30 rare training instances and 17 rare test instances. In that
regime the measurement itself is the binding constraint. An AP computed over one
or two instances is a step function, so the experiment can exclude a large
rare-class effect while being unable to resolve a small one.

The qualitative evidence suggests the mechanism. A pretrained diffusion model
asked to draw a German Versammlungsraum produces perspective furniture, as
Figure~\ref{fig:qastrategya} shows. The LoRA adapter that fixed the domain
mismatch learned mostly that floor plans are white space, as
Figure~\ref{fig:loraval} shows. Part of the reason is that the mask construction
protects detected dark strokes, and the printed room labels and symbols that
identify a room type are exactly those strokes. The safety machinery that made
the augmentation trustworthy also removed most of what carried the class signal.

\subsection*{Future scope}

Four directions follow from the measurements rather than from speculation,
ordered by expected value per unit of effort.

\emph{Read the drawing's own text.} Every plan in this dataset prints the room
type inside the room, such as ``B\"uro'', ``Technik'' or ``Abstellraum''. The
vision-only classifier scored zero recall on all six rare classes while that
label sat inside the crop it was given. Pairing the class-agnostic detector,
which already works, with OCR or a vision-language model over the crop is the
obvious next experiment, and WAFFLE \cite{ganon2025waffle} demonstrates the
approach at scale on heterogeneous floor plans. This is likely to move the
multiclass number more than anything attempted in this project.

\emph{Try a loss-side long-tail treatment before any further data synthesis.}
The project spent its effort on the data and none on the objective. A Seesaw
loss \cite{wang2021seesaw} costs one configuration change, needs no generator,
and attacks the classification imbalance that the two-stage experiment
identified as the actual failure. It should be run on the SAM 3 backbone, where
the headroom is.

\emph{Fix the semantic gap in generation, or steer instead of adapting.} If
generative augmentation is revisited, the literature already identifies two
concrete fixes. Gen-n-Val \cite{huang2025gennval} shows that a semantic
validator, not more generation, is the binding constraint. Our pipeline could
certify structural safety while recording semantic correctness as unverified for
every prompt-based sample. DoGE \cite{wang2024doge} avoids adapter training
entirely by steering a frozen generator with a domain-gap embedding, which suits
a 30-instance tail better than LoRA fine-tuning does. The revised generator
implemented late in this project, which masks compact interior labels while
preserving structural lines and seeds generation with a real rare exemplar, is
built but was never run through the full grid. It is the cheapest of these to
complete. TMI \cite{song2026tmi} additionally predicts that the prompt-driven
and exemplar-driven families are complementary and that neither works alone,
which matches what was observed here and makes the combination worth one run.

\emph{Collect more rare-class test data before running anything else.} This is
the least interesting recommendation and the most important one. Seventeen rare
test instances cannot support the claim the project was designed to make, and no
amount of methodological care removes that ceiling. Roughly a hundred rare test
instances, reachable by annotating a few dozen further plans from the same
archive, would make the difference between excluding a large effect and
measuring a real one. Until that exists, the defensible use of this dataset is
class-agnostic room extraction, where both backbones already perform well, with
room typing handled by the printed labels rather than by appearance.
