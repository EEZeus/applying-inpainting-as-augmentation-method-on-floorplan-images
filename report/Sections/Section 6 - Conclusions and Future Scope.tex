\section{Conclusions and Future Scope}

The project set out to determine whether diffusion or LoRA inpainting of rare
room types improves room-instance segmentation of architectural floor plans, and
whether the answer depends on the backbone. The measured answer is that it does
not improve segmentation, and that the backbone matters far more than the
augmentation. What did improve room classification was reading the room names
printed on the plans.

Goal 1 was achieved as specified. Fourteen controlled arms were built on
generators that read only frozen training images, with each accepted sample
passing automated geometry, pixel-quality and structural checks and carrying a
recorded audit. Each arm trained under the same model, schedule and step budget
in both views. The matrix included the non-generative controls that make the
diffusion treatment interpretable, and that design choice, rather than any
positive finding, produced the project's most useful result.

Goal 2 was achieved in the sense its statement anticipated. A defensible answer
was obtained, and the answer is a null result. Across 84 Mask R-CNN training
runs, no augmentation arm produced a difference from the unaugmented baseline
that the data supports. Every paired sign test on the three-seed grid returned
$p \ge 0.25$, the floor of the test at $n=3$. Nine of the thirteen augmented arms scored below baseline on the class-agnostic track, with Strategy A plus crops and weighting ($-0.065$) and the LoRA conversion ($-0.057$) the worst. The cross-validation over 21 held-out images reproduced the
same ordering. The single result with a consistent sign is exemplar copy-paste
at $+0.045$ class-agnostic AP, positive in all five folds at $p = 0.0625$, and
copy-paste is the control arm that contains no generated pixels. That outcome runs against the hypothesis under test, and Ghiasi et al.\ \cite{ghiasi2021copypaste} would have predicted it. Goal 5 shows that even this signal was inflated by the evaluation.

Goal 3 changed the framing of the project. SAM 3 without any augmentation
reached $0.713$ class-agnostic and $0.115$ multiclass mask AP, exceeding the
best Mask R-CNN arm by $0.089$ and $0.011$ respectively. The class-agnostic gap
is about two and a half times the largest augmentation delta measured anywhere
in the grid. Under augmentation SAM 3 showed a $+0.032$ multiclass gain from
copy-paste, but on a single seed, inside the $0.002$ to $0.018$ seed-noise band
measured on the grid, and accompanied by a class-agnostic loss under all three
augmentations. The sign of the copy-paste effect is therefore opposite in the two backbones, but the augmented SAM 3 runs also received about twice the optimiser steps of their baseline, so these single runs cannot show whether the augmentation effect depends on the backbone.

Goal 4 supplied the diagnosis. Handed perfect ground-truth crops, with no
detector error whatsoever, a frozen DINOv2-base encoder with a linear head
yielded $0.454$ top-1 accuracy over 19 classes and exactly $0.000$ macro recall
over the six primary-rare classes, in every arm and for both heads. Adding 171 rare-class crops, 74 of them synthetic, lowered top-1 accuracy in both heads and left rare recall at zero. The end-to-end two-stage system reached $0.091$ mask AP, below
both fine-tuned detectors. Combined with the class-agnostic results, this
locates the failure precisely. Room localisation is largely solved on this
dataset at $0.59$ to $0.71$ AP, while room typing is not, for the tail and for
several head classes as well. No augmentation of the training pixels was going to fix a classification problem of that shape.

Goal 5 settled what the first four goals could not. Re-scoring all 84 grid
checkpoints without validation-selection leakage shrank the copy-paste gain
from $+0.036$ to $+0.011$ class-agnostic AP and showed five arms (Strategy A
with crops, feather blending and both LoRA arms) to be measurably harmful to
room localisation. The pre-registered confirmation with 12 fresh seeds per arm
then supported neither hypothesis: copy-paste gained $+0.011$ (95\,\% CI
$-0.007$ to $+0.029$), and exemplar inpainting with a three-latent-cell
diffusion band and exposure-parity sampling changed AP by $-0.004$ (95\,\% CI
$-0.019$ to $+0.009$). The inpainting result is the more informative of the
two, because its upper bound lies below the pre-registered smallest effect of
interest, so a practically relevant class-agnostic gain is excluded rather than
merely undetected. An exploratory contrast suggests that what remains of the
copy-paste effect tracks the synthetic dose, not the exemplar pixels.
A second pre-registered study re-tested the multiclass signals. Strategy A
led the baseline by $+0.006$ AP with a one-sided seed-level $p = 0.034$ and
the same sign in all three held-out buildings, but most of the gain came from
one building and the interval over seeds and images ($-0.004$ to $+0.019$)
includes zero, so it does not meet the pre-registered criterion. It is the
only place in the project where generated pixels point in a positive direction.
The gain is small and unconfirmed, and it comes from frequent classes.
Rare-class AP stayed below $0.002$ in every arm.

Goal 6 found the information the earlier classifiers were missing. OCR finds a
label token in 87\,\% of the held-out rooms, and on ground-truth rooms the
text features raise classification accuracy from $0.452$ with DINOv2 features
alone to $0.729$ with both. The pre-registered study still returned three null
results. The text branch, trained on the detector's schedule, barely moved from
its zero initialisation, and label replacement produced only 34 admitted rooms,
because most rare-class names do not fit inside the frequent rooms they would
replace. With its own AdamW optimiser the same branch raised multiclass mask AP
from $0.100$ to $0.184$ (95\,\% CI of the gain $+0.070$ to $+0.112$), with
the largest gains on rooms with distinctive names such as K\"uche and
Besprechungsraum. A text classifier
over class-agnostic SAM 3 masks reached $0.258$ on the test plans against
$0.115$ for SAM 3 alone. Both results come from the exploratory follow-up and
need a fresh confirmation. The same follow-up closed the question of synthetic
dose: copy-paste that brought each rare class to 63 to 95 training instances
lowered multiclass AP by $0.017$ and raised rare AP only to $0.005$. Synthetic
rare rooms helped in one place, the two-stage text classifier, where they
raised rare AP from $0.099$ to $0.126$. The classifier behind the $0.258$ on
SAM 3 masks was trained with them.

The wider conclusion is a scope statement rather than a defeat. The generative
augmentation literature surveyed in Section~3 reports its gains on benchmarks
with 100k training images, where a rare class still has a few hundred instances
\cite{gupta2019lvis,xie2024mosaicfusion,shin2023fillup}. This project had 35
training plans, 30 rare training instances and 17 rare test instances. In that
regime the measurement itself is the binding constraint. An AP computed over one
or two instances is a step function, so the experiment can exclude a large
rare-class effect while being unable to resolve a small one.

The qualitative evidence suggests the mechanism. A pretrained diffusion model
asked to draw a German Versammlungsraum produces perspective furniture, as
Figure~\ref{fig:qastrategya} shows. The LoRA adapter that fixed the domain
mismatch learned mostly that floor plans are white space, as
Figure~\ref{fig:loraval} shows. Part of the reason is that the mask construction
protects detected dark strokes, and the printed room labels and symbols that
identify a room type are exactly those strokes. The safety machinery that made
the augmentation trustworthy also removed most of what carried the class signal.
Section~\ref{sec:ocr} confirms that this signal is the printed name: a model
that reads it gains more than any augmentation, and a model that only sees the
plan at detector resolution cannot use it.

\subsection*{Future scope}

Six directions follow from the measurements rather than from speculation,
ordered by expected value per unit of effort.

\emph{Confirm the text model and move it to SAM 3.} The gain of the text
branch comes from an exploratory arm on held-out plans that earlier arms had
already used. It needs a confirmation on fresh seeds and, ideally, on plans
from buildings outside the current split. The SAM 3 result suggests that the
branch belongs on the stronger backbone, either inside its classifier or as the
two-stage text classifier used here. WAFFLE \cite{ganon2025waffle} reads
floor-plan text at a much larger scale and is the closest reference.

\emph{Handle names that training never saw.} Rare-class AP stays below $0.13$
even when text is read, because many rare rooms carry names such as
``Spindraum'' that do not occur in the training plans. The zero-shot similarity
between a name and the class names already recalls more rare rooms than any
trained head. A lexicon of German room names per class, or a language model
asked to map a printed name to a class, would extend this without new
annotation.

\emph{Try a loss-side long-tail treatment before any further data synthesis.}
The project spent its effort on the data and none on the objective. A Seesaw
loss \cite{wang2021seesaw} costs one configuration change, needs no generator,
and attacks the classification imbalance that the two-stage experiment
identified as the actual failure. It should be run on the SAM 3 backbone, where
the headroom is.

\emph{Revisit generation only with the text in view.} Label replacement was
limited by space: a rare-class name rarely fits inside the frequent room it
replaces, and the diffusion fill failed the erasure check in most rooms. A
generator that chooses target rooms by the size of the name to be printed, or
abbreviates names as the plans themselves do (``Abst.'', ``Putzr.''), would
raise the yield. Gen-n-Val \cite{huang2025gennval} and DoGE \cite{wang2024doge}
remain the relevant references for validating and steering the generator.

\emph{Fix the training protocol.} Every Mask R-CNN arm trained for 875
optimiser steps at batch size 1 with plain BatchNorm, and the learning rate was
cut tenfold after 280 of them. The text branch showed how much this schedule can
hide: the same branch gave nothing on it and $+0.085$ AP with its own
optimiser. A longer schedule should be the default for any further comparison,
together with frozen BatchNorm or a larger batch.

\emph{Collect more rare-class test data before running anything else.} This is
the least interesting recommendation and the most important one. Seventeen rare
test instances cannot support the claim the project was designed to make, and no
amount of methodological care removes that ceiling. Roughly a hundred rare test
instances, reachable by annotating a few dozen further plans from the same
archive, would make the difference between excluding a large effect and
measuring a real one. Until that exists, the defensible use of this dataset is
class-agnostic room extraction, where both backbones already perform well, with
room classification taken from the printed labels, as Section~\ref{sec:ocr}
supports.
