\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Every number below was read from the consolidated summary files under
\protect\path{outputs/final/}, and each table names its source. Deltas are stated with
the sample size $n$, the standard deviation of the underlying repetitions, and
the $p$-value of the paired test against the unaugmented arm e0. Where a delta
is not significant it is called a difference rather than an improvement.

The class-agnostic track is the more legible half of the grid, because its
standard deviations are proportionally much smaller. The baseline is

The multiclass picture is unchanged. The six arms span $0.134$ to $0.154$
against a baseline of $0.143 \pm 0.037$, the two diffusion-based arms are the
two lowest, and no paired test falls below $p = 0.375$. Overall AP is 1.6 to 1.8 times its grid value in every arm. That reflects the different composition of
the evaluation set rather than any change in the models, since the folds include the former validation images, which come from a project whose plans the detectors find easier and on which these checkpoints had been selected.

The class-agnostic column holds the one result in the project with a consistent
sign. Copy-paste scores $0.647 \pm 0.061$ against $0.602 \pm 0.044$, a
difference of $+0.045$ that is positive in all five folds
($\{+0.070, +0.033, +0.016, +0.040, +0.067\}$). The exact two-sided sign test
gives $p = 0.0625$, the smallest value obtainable with $K = 5$, so it cannot
clear $0.05$ however consistent the effect is. That ceiling is a limit of the
design and is stated as such. The remaining class-agnostic arms are further from
significance still, at $p = 1.000$ for e1 and e10 and $p = 0.375$ for e6 and for
the negative $-0.034$ of e12. The honest summary is that copy-paste shows a
small positive effect that replicated across every fold and across both Mask
R-CNN evaluations, and that the evidence is suggestive rather than conclusive. The cross-validation scores only seed 42's selected checkpoints on folds that contain the selection images; the pre-registered, selection-free replication in Section~\ref{sec:confirm} reduces the copy-paste gain to $+0.011$ AP with a confidence interval that includes zero.
Figure~\ref{fig:cvagnostic} shows the fold structure directly.

Pooling the folds also produces the first non-zero rare numbers in the project.
They deserve a precise statement, because they look better than they are. The
pooled rare-macro AP is $0.050$ for e10 and $0.029$ for e8, against $0.000$ for
e0, e1, e6 and e12. In both cases the entire value comes from one category.
Abstellraum, with a pooled support of three instances, reaches a pooled mask AP
of $0.300$ for e10 and $0.176$ for e8. The other five primary-rare categories
score exactly $0.000$ in every arm, including Allg.\ Verkehrsfl\"ache with the
largest pooled rare support of 13 instances. A rare-macro AP of $0.05$ driven by
three instances of one class says nothing about whether inpainting augmentation
improves rare-class segmentation.

On the multiclass track SAM 3 improved under all three augmentations, by
$+0.005$ for diffusion inpaint, $+0.026$ for the LoRA conversion and $+0.032$
for copy-paste. The last is the largest positive augmentation effect measured
anywhere in the project. It is still not evidence of an effect. Each value comes
from a single run, and the seed-to-seed standard deviation on the Mask R-CNN
multiclass grid ranges from $0.002$ to $0.018$, with individual arms spanning up
to $0.041$ across their three seeds. All three SAM 3 deltas lie inside that
band, so one plausible explanation is the draw of a single seed. Calling them
improvements would require the three-seed repetition that queue time did not
allow.

On the class-agnostic track the direction reverses. Every augmentation lowered
SAM 3's AP, from $0.713$ at baseline to $0.704$, $0.693$ and $0.685$. The sign
of the copy-paste effect is therefore opposite in the two backbones, at $+0.036$
for Mask R-CNN and $-0.020$ for SAM 3, which is itself an argument against reading any of these single-run deltas as a property of the augmentation. The augmented SAM 3 runs also received about twice the optimiser steps of their baseline and were scored at their last checkpoint, so these deltas are confounded as well as unreplicated.

The one comparison in Table~\ref{tab:sam3} that is not fragile is the vertical
one. Without augmentation, SAM 3 exceeds the best Mask R-CNN arm by $0.089$
class-agnostic AP ($0.713$ against $0.624$) and by $0.011$ multiclass AP
($0.115$ against $0.104$). The class-agnostic gap is roughly two and a half
times the largest positive augmentation delta in the grid and about four times the typical one. Whatever benefit augmentation might carry at
this data scale, backbone choice dominates it.

Evaluation A is the diagnostic result of the project. Given perfect ground-truth
crops, with no detector error at all, a frozen DINOv2-base encoder with a linear

Adding the 171 rare-class crops, 74 of them synthetic, did not help. Top-1 accuracy fell from
$0.454$ to $0.440$ for the linear head and from $0.386$ to $0.340$ for the kNN
head, head-macro recall fell in both, and rare-macro recall stayed at $0.000$.
The synthetic crops came from the same pipeline whose output the detectors also
declined to benefit from, so the result is consistent rather than surprising.
What it does is close off the hypothesis that the crops were useful data the
detector setting had simply failed to exploit. The single non-zero rare number
in the table, a two-stage rare AP of $0.0042$, belongs to the arm with the worst
overall accuracy of the four, and its rare-macro recall is still zero.

Evaluation B places the pipeline on the same axis as the fine-tuned detectors.
Its best configuration reaches $0.0910$ two-stage mask AP, below fine-tuned SAM
3 at $0.115$ and below the best Mask R-CNN arm at $0.104$, and above the unaugmented Mask R-CNN baseline at $0.081$ and several augmented arms. Figure~\ref{fig:dinotwostage} shows
this against the three reference lines. Splitting the problem in two therefore

$0.713$ class-agnostic AP, and the two-stage system converts that into $0.091$
multiclass AP. The gap between those two numbers is the classification problem,
measured end to end.

\subsection*{Summary of the evidence}

Reading the four experiments together, three statements are supported and one
common expectation is not.

Diffusion and LoRA inpainting augmentation did not reliably improve floor-plan
room segmentation. On Mask R-CNN the effect is neutral to harmful, and it
replicated as such across three seeds and five cross-validation folds. Nine of
thirteen augmented arms fall below baseline on the class-agnostic grid, the two
diffusion-based arms are the two lowest multiclass arms in the
cross-validation, and the LoRA arm is the second-worst arm in the grid at $-0.057$. On SAM 3 there is a small multiclass gain that is unreplicated, single-seed, confounded by step count and inside the noise band measured on the grid, accompanied by a class-agnostic loss
under every augmentation. As classifier-crop augmentation the synthetic data
lowered every metric it touched.

Simple exemplar copy-paste, the control arm containing no generated pixels, is
the only augmentation with a small and consistent positive effect. It is the
top-ranked arm in three of the six backbone and track comparisons run here: the
class-agnostic grid, the class-agnostic cross-validation, and SAM 3 multiclass.
It is not top-ranked in the multiclass grid, where e7 leads, in the multiclass
cross-validation, where e1 leads, or on the SAM 3 class-agnostic track, where
the baseline leads. Its strongest single result is the cross-validated $+0.045$ class-agnostic AP,
positive in all five folds at $p = 0.0625$. That result rests on the
contaminated cross-validation, and Section~\ref{sec:confirm} shows that it does
not survive a pre-registered, selection-free replication at the conventional
threshold.

The backbone dominates the augmentation. Without any augmentation, SAM 3 exceeds
the best Mask R-CNN arm by $0.089$ class-agnostic AP and $0.011$ multiclass AP,
and the class-agnostic figure is larger than every augmentation delta measured
in the project.

What the evidence does not support is the premise the project set out to test.
Rare-class room segmentation is not merely unimproved by augmentation. It is out
of reach at this data scale for every model that does not read the printed room names. Rare AP sits at or near zero
across all fourteen arms, both backbones and both evaluation protocols, and
rare-macro recall is exactly zero even when a frozen classifier receives perfect
ground-truth crops. With 17 rare test instances, or 40 pooled over the
cross-validation, the experiment can exclude a large effect and cannot resolve a
small one. Class-agnostic localisation, meanwhile, already works, at $0.588$ to
$0.647$ AP for Mask R-CNN and $0.713$ for SAM 3. The unsolved component is
room-type classification, and a dedicated frozen classifier did not fix it.
Section~\ref{sec:confirm} re-tests the surviving augmentation signals without
the evaluation's confounds, and Section~\ref{sec:ocr} returns to the
classification component with the room names printed on the plans.
