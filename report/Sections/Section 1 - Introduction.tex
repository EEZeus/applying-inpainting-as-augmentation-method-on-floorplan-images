\section{Introduction}

Architectural floor plans encode a building as a set of labelled rooms. Turning
a scanned or exported plan back into that structured form --- one polygon and one
room type per room --- is an instance-segmentation problem, and it is the step
that stands between a drawing archive and any downstream query about areas,
circulation or fire-safety compliance. Public work on the task has concentrated
on residential plans with a handful of coarse categories
\cite{kalervo2019cubicasa5k,kratochvila2024multiunit}. The dataset used in this
project is different in a way that matters: it consists of German
\emph{Feuerwehrpl\"ane} (fire-brigade plans) of office and institutional
buildings, annotated with 19 functional room categories whose frequencies span
almost three orders of magnitude. Six of those categories --- Allg.\
Verkehrsfl\"ache, Abstellraum, Versammlungsraum, Schacht, Garderobe and
Sanit\"arraum --- together account for 70 of the 1633 polygon annotations, of which 30 fall in
the training split and only 17 in the held-out test split.

That imbalance is the practical obstacle. A detector trained on the raw data
learns the frequent categories and never predicts the rare ones, so the very
rooms an inspector most often needs to locate are the ones the model cannot
name. The generic remedy in the long-tailed literature is to re-weight the loss
or the sampler \cite{wang2021seesaw}; the more recent remedy is to synthesise the
missing examples with a text-to-image diffusion model
\cite{xie2024mosaicfusion,shin2023fillup,wang2024doge}. Floor plans are an
unusually favourable setting for the synthetic route, because the drawing is
mostly white space bounded by thin black strokes: a room interior can be
repainted while every wall, door, label and neighbouring room is preserved
bit-exactly. The project therefore asked a narrow, testable question. Does
diffusion or LoRA inpainting of rare rooms improve room-instance segmentation on
this dataset, and does the answer depend on which backbone is used?

The work reported here consists of four parts, all carried out by the author. A
leakage-safe augmentation stack was built that generates rare-room examples from
training-split pixels only and admits them through automated structural and
pixel-level quality control, producing fourteen controlled experiment arms
covering prompt-based inpainting, an exemplar-guided variant with copy-paste and
feather-blend ablations, and a class-conditioned rank-8 LoRA adapter
\cite{hu2022lora} on Stable Diffusion 1.5 inpainting \cite{rombach2022ldm}.
Each arm was then trained and evaluated twice --- once for the 19-class problem
and once for a derived class-agnostic ``room versus background'' view --- with a
torchvision Mask R-CNN \cite{he2017maskrcnn} over three seeds, and re-evaluated
under a five-fold rotated held-out cross-validation that pools 21 held-out
images instead of 11. The three augmentation datasets that looked most promising
were carried over to a fine-tuned SAM 3 \cite{carion2025sam3}. Finally, a
two-stage pipeline was implemented that separates localisation from naming: SAM 3
class-agnostic proposals are cropped and classified by a frozen DINOv2-base
encoder \cite{oquab2024dinov2} with a linear or cosine-kNN head, with and without
the synthetic rare crops.

The result is negative and consistent. Across three seeds and five
cross-validation folds, no diffusion or LoRA arm beat the unaugmented baseline
by a margin the data can support; every paired sign test on the three-seed grid
returned $p \ge 0.25$, and nine of thirteen augmented arms scored below baseline
on the class-agnostic track. The only augmentation with a repeatable positive
sign was plain exemplar copy-paste, which is the control condition containing no
generated pixels at all, and even its best case ($+0.045$ class-agnostic AP,
positive in all five folds) reaches only $p = 0.0625$, the smallest $p$-value
attainable at $K = 5$. Rare-class AP remained at or near zero everywhere. What
did move the numbers was the choice of backbone: SAM 3 scored $0.713$
class-agnostic mask AP against $0.624$ for the best Mask R-CNN arm, a gap larger
than every augmentation effect measured in the project combined. The
diagnostic value of the two-stage experiment is what makes the negative result
informative rather than merely disappointing --- the frozen DINOv2 classifier
recalled \emph{zero} rare instances even when handed perfect ground-truth crops,
which locates the failure in room-type classification under $17$--$40$ test
instances rather than in the segmentation model or in the augmentation pipeline.
