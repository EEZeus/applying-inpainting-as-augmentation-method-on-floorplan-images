\section{Problem statement and Goals}

The dataset consists of 56 distinct German fire-brigade floor plans carrying
1633 polygon annotations over 19 functional room categories. A frozen
project-aware split assigns 35 plans to training, 10 to validation and 11 to
test, and no building project crosses a partition boundary. The class
frequencies are strongly long-tailed. Categories 1--10 hold 1551 of the 1633
annotations. The six categories designated primary-rare (11 Allg.\
Verkehrsfl\"ache, 12 Abstellraum, 13 Versammlungsraum, 14 Schacht, 15 Garderobe,
16 Sanit\"arraum) hold 70 annotations, 30 of them in training and 17 in test.
Two further categories, 17 Werkstatt and 18 Speiseraum, have too little support to score and are excluded from quantitative claims. Category 19 Labor has no test instances at
all and is excluded from every quantitative claim. Section~\ref{sec:dataset}
gives the per-split counts and Figure~\ref{fig:classdist} plots them.

A detector trained on this distribution learns the head classes and assigns
almost no probability mass to the tail. The room types of greatest operational
interest, such as shafts and storage rooms, are located but never named, or not
detected at all. This affects the archive owners who would use such a model to
convert legacy plans into structured building data, along with any later
fire-safety or facility-management query that depends on room type rather than
room geometry.

The drawings are line art on white ground, so a single room interior can be
repainted by a diffusion model while every wall stroke, door symbol, text label
and neighbouring annotation is preserved. That makes the dataset a clean testbed
for the hypothesis that generative augmentation can fill a long tail. The
project was formulated so that a negative answer would still be informative.
Generation reads training-split pixels only, so no synthetic sample can leak
validation or test information. Every arm differs from the baseline in exactly
one controlled respect. Any reported effect has to survive repetition over seeds
and over evaluation splits. The aim was therefore to measure, with a defensible
error bar, whether inpainting augmentation helps, and whether the answer belongs
to the augmentation or to the backbone.

Two evaluation views are used throughout. The \emph{multiclass} view is the
19-category instance-segmentation problem scored with COCO mask AP
\cite{lin2014coco}, together with a rare-macro AP averaged over categories
11--16 that have test support. The \emph{class-agnostic} view maps every
annotation to a single \texttt{room} category and measures whether the model
finds room boundaries at all, independent of naming. Separating the two views
allows a change in localisation to be told apart from a change in
classification, and it turned out to be the decisive measurement of the project.

\subsection{Goals}

\subsubsection{Goal 1: Leakage-safe augmented datasets and a controlled experiment matrix}

Build a generation stack that produces additional rare-room training examples
without ever reading a validation or test image, then wrap it in an experiment
matrix in which every arm is comparable to the unaugmented baseline. Success
here is verifiable rather than statistical. Every generator must be provably
restricted to the frozen training images. Every accepted sample must pass
automated geometry, pixel-quality and structural checks with a recorded audit.
Every arm must use the same model, optimiser schedule and step budget, and must
be evaluated on the untouched test annotations. The matrix must also isolate the
treatment of interest, diffusion inpainting, against non-generative controls, so
that any measured effect can be attributed to the generated pixels and not to
the surrounding pipeline.

\subsubsection{Goal 2: Measure the augmentation effect on Mask R-CNN}

Quantify the effect of each augmentation arm on a torchvision Mask R-CNN, in
both views, with an error bar large enough to support or reject a claim. This
needs two independent repetitions. The first is a grid over three random seeds
$\{42, 123, 2026\}$ for all fourteen arms. The second re-evaluates the frozen
checkpoints on five disjoint folds of the 21 held-out images, which raises the
pooled rare test support from 17 to 40 instances. Success is a statistically
defensible AP delta relative to the unaugmented arm e0. That explicitly includes
the outcome that no defensible delta exists, in which case the reported
conclusion is a null result stated with its $n$, standard deviation and
$p$-value.

\subsubsection{Goal 3: Test whether a foundation-model backbone changes the verdict}

Determine whether the Mask R-CNN verdict belongs to the augmentation or to the
model class, by repeating the comparison on SAM 3 with its official trainer,
open-vocabulary text-concept head and the unchanged split. The three most
informative augmentation datasets from Goal 2 are re-used verbatim, so the only
difference between the two backbones is the backbone itself. Success is a direct
statement of whether the sign and magnitude of the augmentation effect survive,
plus a measurement of the backbone effect, which then serves as the scale
against which every augmentation delta is judged.

\subsubsection{Goal 4: Test a two-stage DINOv2 pipeline and inpainting crops as classifier augmentation}

Separate localisation from naming by pairing a class-agnostic detector with a
frozen DINOv2-base classifier over crop features, then test whether the
inpainting crops help the classifier stage where they did not help the detector.
Two evaluations are needed. One classifies ground-truth regions, which removes
detector error entirely and isolates the classification bottleneck. The other
measures end-to-end two-stage mask AP, which can be compared directly against
the fine-tuned detectors on the same test split. Success is a diagnosis: whether
the residual error is a localisation problem or a classification problem, and whether synthetic crops move the classification number in either direction.

\subsubsection{Goal 5: Confirm the surviving signals without the evaluation's confounds}

A close audit of the first four goals' evaluation found confounds that could
hide or inflate an augmentation effect: the cross-validation reused the plans
that had selected each checkpoint, augmented arms saw each real plan less
often than the baseline, and the diffusion arm regenerated only a band about
one latent cell wide. Goal 5 repairs these and re-tests the surviving signals
in a pre-registered study with fresh seeds. Success is a confirmatory answer
with a confidence interval, whichever way it falls.
