\section{Confirmatory Study}
\label{sec:confirm}

The original evaluation could not separate a real augmentation effect from its own confounds: the rotated cross-validation reused the validation plans that had selected each checkpoint, augmented arms saw each real plan less often than the baseline, the copy-paste and diffusion arms were built from differently composed datasets, and the diffusion arm regenerated a band about one latent cell wide. This section reports a study designed to remove those confounds. Its hypotheses, endpoint, gates and tests were fixed in \code{docs/preregistration_confirm.md} and committed before any job ran.

\noindent Pre-registration record: \code{783f6090e81aafa1ca40aae0069aa3bdc9647c46} (commit), recorded in \code{outputs/confirm/preregistration_record.txt}.

\subsection{Protocol}
\begin{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Endpoint.} class-agnostic COCO mask AP@[.50:.95] of the epoch-25 checkpoint (last.pth), which is never selected on validation data, evaluated on all 21 held-out plans (10 validation + 11 test plans, projects P02, P04 and P07).
  \item \textbf{Arms.} e0 original plans; e8 exact replication of the grid's copy-paste arm; e16 matched copy-paste; e17 matched wide-seam exemplar inpainting. e16 and e17 are composed from the same (target, donor) pairs with the same builder seed, so they differ only in pixels.
  \item \textbf{Exposure parity.} e16 and e17 use a paired-variant sampler: every epoch follows e0's plan order for the same seed, so every source plan occupies exactly one step per epoch, as in e0; a plan that has synthetic variants is shown as one of them with probability 0.5. Arms with the same seed therefore see the same plan sequence and differ only in the pixels of the swapped steps.
  \item \textbf{Wide seam.} the diffusion band is 24 model pixels (3 Stable Diffusion latent cells) per candidate; the donor's dark strokes (labels, symbols) are excluded from the diffusion mask and checked by the retention QC.
  \item \textbf{Seeds and tests.} 12 fresh seeds (1001-1012) per arm. H1 e8 > e0 and H2 e17 > e0 by one-sided Welch tests with Holm correction; a hypothesis is supported only if Holm rejects and the two-level (seed $\times$ image) bootstrap 95\% CI excludes zero. H3 e17 > e16 is tested only if H2 is supported.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interpretation of the class-agnostic confirmation}

No confirmatory hypothesis is supported. The study removes the confounds of the original grid and replaces its uninterpretable sign tests with interval estimates: every pre-registered effect is bounded above by +0.029 AP.

For copy-paste (H1) the interval [-0.007, +0.029] still admits an effect of +0.02; the point estimate +0.011 is positive in 3 of 3 held-out buildings, so the data neither confirm nor exclude a small benefit.

For wide-seam exemplar inpainting (H2) the upper 95\% bound is +0.009 AP, below the pre-registered smallest effect of interest (+0.02); a practically relevant gain is therefore excluded, not merely undetected.

For the generated pixels (H3) (descriptive, not formally tested) the upper 95\% bound is +0.018 AP, below the pre-registered smallest effect of interest (+0.02); a practically relevant gain is therefore excluded, not merely undetected.

Exploratory: e8 exceeds the exposure-parity copy-paste arm e16 by +0.018 AP (95\% CI [-0.000, +0.036], one-sided p = 0.020). e8 keeps the original sampler and saw 2.2 times as many converted-room occurrences per run (558 against 256), drawn from a slightly larger pool. The difference therefore points at synthetic dose, not at the exemplar pixels as such, as the driver of the original copy-paste gain; it was not a pre-registered contrast.

Exploratory re-score, multiclass track: arms whose interval excludes zero are e1, e2, e7. Rare-weighted sampling alone (e1, no generated pixels) already gains +0.010, while Strategy A (e2) gains +0.016; the part attributable to inpainted appearance is at most about +0.006, consistent with the oversampling confound of the fixed step budget. These are three-seed numbers that re-use the seeds which produced the original ranking; Section~\ref{sec:confirmmc} re-tests them with fresh seeds.

Exploratory re-score, class-agnostic track: arms whose interval lies entirely below e0 are e4, e5, e9, e12, e13; these augmentations measurably harm room localisation.

Limitations: the endpoint is class-agnostic only, because 17 rare test instances cannot resolve a rare-class effect; the 21 held-out plans come from three buildings; BatchNorm runs at batch size 1 in every arm; the synthetic pool is small (one to two converted rooms per synthetic plan, from at most 52 planned pairs).

\subsection{Multiclass follow-up: Strategy A versus oversampling}
\label{sec:confirmmc}

The exploratory re-score left one open question: Strategy A (e2) and rare-weighted sampling (e1) both improved multiclass AP, and e1 adds no generated pixels. A second pre-registered study (\code{docs/preregistration_confirm_mc.md}) trained e0, e1 and e2 on the multiclass track with 12 fresh seeds each and scored last.pth on the 21 held-out plans. H4 tests e2 > e0; H5, tested only if H4 is supported, tests e2 > e1, i.e. whether inpainted appearance adds anything beyond oversampling.

Rare-class macro mask AP: e0 0.0000, e1 0.0012, e2 0.0018. It stays near zero in every arm, so any change in overall AP comes from the frequent classes, not from the rare classes the augmentation targets.

The seed-level test alone would clear $p < 0.05$. The pre-registered rule also
requires the interval that accounts for which plans happen to be held out to
exclude zero, and it does not: most of the gain comes from one building (P07,
$+0.017$; P02 and P04 about $+0.002$ each). Strategy A therefore shows a small
multiclass improvement that is consistent in sign but not established at this
test-set size. The fresh seeds also do not reproduce the exploratory
oversampling gain ($+0.010$ in the re-score, $+0.002$ here), so the earlier
reading that Strategy A's gain is mostly oversampling is not confirmed either;
the difference between e2 and e1 ($+0.004$) remains unresolved.
